### Reference implementation

The first run found 74 mismatching lists on test (trapped 70, hanging 2, overloaded 2)
and 73 on dev. Two detector causes, both fixed to the text (M2 §15):
- SEE took the first of two equally valuable least attackers. Test `CkZLj`: …Rbxa3
  keeps the b2 pawn pinned and wins the bishop; …Raxa3 releases the pin.
- `trapped_piece` on the null-move board counted a pseudo-capture of the king as a
  legal escape (test `dLgSM`).
§4 now says that SEE tries every equally valuable least attacker. §5 now says that a
terminal board has no state motifs at any ply. Both implementations already did this.

### Recall-miss audit (§8.3, test)

| Tag | Definition narrower than the chess term | Lichess label doubtful | Implementation deviates from §4 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fork | 5 | 1 | 0 | 6 |
| pin | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| skewer | 6 | 4 | 0 | 10 |
| discoveredAttack | 10 | 0 | 0 | 10 |

- **Narrower definitions (22):**
  - fork: the forking piece can be captured (`see(after, b, Y) > 0`), e.g. `KQf2R` Nf6
    taken by …Bxf6.
  - skewer: the rear piece is a pawn (5), or the line is opened by a pawn move (1).
  - discoveredAttack: the target is a pawn (4), or an equal or less valuable piece
    with SEE 0 (6).
  - pin: the pin stands on the checkmated board (`hGd6B`); per §5, a terminal board has
    no state motifs.
- **Lichess label doubtful (5):** for example `HMHew`, where Qe4+ checks along one ray
  and attacks along another: a fork, not a skewer.
- **Implementation bugs:** none. The audit's single candidate (`hGd6B`) is the terminal
  rule, and the reference implementation agrees with the detector.
