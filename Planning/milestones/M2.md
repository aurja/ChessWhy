## 4. Motif definitions (v0)

Credit: these definitions are our own text, written after reading lichess-puzzler
(`tagger/cook.py`, `tagger/util.py` at commit `608cf7f2b2a2a02682736ff176b454a30b398e01`,
AGPL-3.0, by Thibault Duplessis and contributors). Where a rule follows lichess-puzzler's
criteria, the row says so. Workers implement from this text only and do not read
lichess-puzzler.

Notation: `X` = the side that benefits from the motif (the motif's `side`), `Y` = the
other side. `value(K)` is greater than every other piece's for comparisons.
`see(board, s, X)` = net material X wins, in pawn units, by the best exchange sequence
on square `s`, X capturing first, both sides may stop, least valuable attacker first,
x-ray attackers behind sliders included, absolutely pinned pieces may capture only along
their pin ray, the king captures only onto a square no longer attacked. It is 0 when X
does not attack `s`.

A **state motif** is a fact about one position. A **move motif** is a fact about one move
(the move from ply k−1 to ply k, made by X).

### 4.1 State motifs

| Tag | Definition | Actor / targets / gain | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hanging_piece` | X is to move; a Y knight, bishop, rook or queen on `s` with `see(board, s, X) ≥ 3`; when X is in check, X also has a legal capture on `s` (SEE ignores check); inside a line, not when that piece made the ply's move as a capture of a piece whose value is ≥ that SEE value (a trade in progress). At ply 0 there is no previous move, so only "X to move" applies | actor: the hanging piece; targets: none; gain: the SEE value | lichess-puzzler uses "not defended"; ours uses SEE (vision §5.5) |
| `pin_absolute` | An X bishop, rook or queen, then along one of its rays (nothing in between) a Y non-king piece `p`, then (nothing in between) the Y king. Rook-type rays for rooks, diagonal rays for bishops, both for queens | actor: pinner; targets: `p`, king | — |
| `pin_relative` | As `pin_absolute`, but the rear piece `q` is a Y non-king piece with `value(q) > value(p)`, and exposing it wins material: `see` on `q`'s square for X with `p` removed ≥ 1 | actor: pinner; targets: `p`, `q` | — |
| `back_rank_weakness` | The Y king stands on Y's first rank; each square directly in front of it or diagonally in front (those on the board) is occupied by a Y piece or attacked by X; X has a rook or queen; Y has no rook or queen on its first rank | actor: the Y king; targets: none | after lichess-puzzler's back-rank mate shape |
| `overloaded_defender` | A Y non-king piece `d`, and at least two Y non-king pieces `t1`, `t2`, … attacked by X, each with `see(board, t, X) ≤ 0` but `≥ 1` on the same board with `d` removed | actor: `d`; targets: the `t` pieces | no lichess-puzzler counterpart (its `overloading` always returns false) |
| `trapped_piece` | A Y knight, bishop, rook or queen on `s` with `see(board, s, X) ≥ 1`, where Y is not in check, and every move of that piece (generated with Y to move: on the null-move board when X is to move) is illegal, or lands on a square `s'` with `see(after, s', X) ≥ 1`, and captures nothing of value ≥ its own. Inside a line (ply ≥ 1) only when a later move of the same line moves or captures that piece | actor: the trapped piece; targets: none; gain: SEE at `s` | after lichess-puzzler's `is_trapped` |

State motifs are computed for both sides (each side once as X).

### 4.2 Move motifs

The move `mv` is made by X from square `a` to square `b` of piece `m`. Castling: `m` is
the king (actor on its destination), and the castling rook also counts as moved, so a
check given by that rook is not a discovered check and the rook is never the unmasked
piece of a discovered attack.

| Tag | Definition | Actor / targets | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fork` | `m` is not a king; after `mv`, `m` attacks at least two Y non-pawn pieces that are each the king, or more valuable than `m`, or a piece `t` with `see(after, t, X) ≥ 1` that does not itself attack `b`; and `see(after, b, Y) ≤ 0` | actor: `m` on `b`; targets: the counted pieces | after lichess-puzzler's `fork` |
| `skewer` | `m` is a bishop, rook or queen; after `mv`, along one of `m`'s rays from `b`: a Y piece `f`, then (nothing in between) a Y non-pawn piece `r`, with `f` the king or `value(f) > value(r)`; and `see(after, b, Y) ≤ 0`; when `f` is not the king, X's SEE on `r`'s square with `f` removed is ≥ 1 | actor: `m`; targets: `f`, `r` | — |
| `discovered_check` | After `mv`, the Y king is in check from an X piece other than `m` | actor: that checker; targets: Y king | after lichess-puzzler's `discovered_check` |
| `discovered_attack` | Not a discovered check; `a` lies strictly between an X bishop, rook or queen `q` and a Y non-pawn piece `t` on one of `q`'s rays, `b` is not on that segment, and after `mv` `q` attacks `t` with `t` more valuable than `q` or `see(after, t, X) ≥ 1` | actor: `q`; targets: `t` (several when one move opens several attacks, one motif per `q`) | — |
| `double_check` | After `mv`, the Y king is attacked by two X pieces | actor: `m`; targets: Y king | after lichess-puzzler's `double_check` |
| `back_rank_mate` | `mv` gives checkmate; the Y king is on Y's first rank; a checker stands on that rank; each square in front of the king (straight and diagonal, on the board) is occupied by a Y piece | actor: the checker on the rank; targets: Y king | after lichess-puzzler's `back_rank_mate` |
| `removal_of_defender` | `mv` captures a Y piece `d`; on the board before `mv` there is a Y non-king piece `t ≠ d` with `see(before, t, X) ≤ 0` and `≥ 1` with `d` removed; X captures on `t`'s square at a later ply of the same walked line, and `see` on that square for X on the board before that capture is ≥ 1 | actor: `m`; targets: `d`, `t` | after lichess-puzzler's `capturing_defender` |
| `promotion` | `mv` is a promotion | actor: the promoted piece on `b`; targets: none | after lichess-puzzler's `promotion` |

Move motifs only exist inside a line (ply ≥ 1). `removal_of_defender` needs the rest of
the walked line, so it is evaluated after the whole line is known.

### 4.3 Not detectors

- **Mate threat**: the engine's consequence class `mate` on a threat (M1 §4.3), source
  `engine`. No rule duplicates it.
- **Promotion threat**: the consequence's `promotes` (M1) plus `promotion` in the line.

## 5. Report v2

- `Motif`: `source` (`rule`), `tag`, `side` (X), `ply`, `actor`, `targets`, `gain`.
  `actor` and each target are `{square, piece}`: square `a1`–`h8`, piece as a FEN letter
  (case gives the colour). `gain` is an integer for `hanging_piece` and `trapped_piece`,
  null otherwise.
- `position.motifs`: state motifs of the current position (ply 0), both sides. Empty
  for terminal positions.
- `Threat.motifs` (threats, `creates_threat`, `refutation`) and `MoveAssessment.motifs`
  (the move's own line): motifs at plies 1..`plies` of that line. `plies` = the walked
  plies (`walked_plies`, M1 §4.3 rule: 6 plies, then while the line keeps capturing, up
  to 10). A state motif is listed at ply k only when it holds after ply k and did not
  hold after ply k−1. Ply 0 is not repeated in lines.
- Motif identity for "did not hold": tag, side, actor, targets.
- Targets within a motif: in the order the §4 actor/targets column names them (pins:
  pinned piece, then rear piece; skewer: `f`, `r`; removal: `d`, `t`); where a motif has
  several targets of one kind (fork, discovered attack, overloaded defender), by square.
- Order: by ply, then tag in §4 table order (state tags first), then actor square
  (a1 = 0 … h8 = 63), then target squares. Output is deterministic.
- A bad move's `motifs` overlap its `refutation.motifs` (shifted by one ply). Both are
  kept; the template picks one (§10).
- `schema_version` 2; `schema/report.v2.schema.json` adds `$defs/motif` and the three
  `motifs` arrays (required, possibly empty). No other field changes.

## 7. Puzzle sample

### 7.1 Source and eligibility

- `assets/data/lichess_db_puzzle.csv.zst`, export of 2026-10-05, SHA-256
  `76335bfa7d7c4a7f93c1366d81549e53951ebb79dd43d34904cab8f22d962f8d` (6,157,341 puzzles).
- Eligible: `RatingDeviation ≤ 80`, `NbPlays ≥ 1000`, `Popularity ≥ 80`, at most 7 moves
  in `Moves` (opponent's move plus a solution of ≤ 6 plies, inside the walk of §5).
  Measured: 1,326,623 eligible puzzles.

### 7.2 Selection

`tools/sample_puzzles.py --split test|dev` (stdlib only, reads the CSV from `zstdcat` on
stdin). Order eligible puzzles by SHA-256 of `chesswhy-m2-<split>:<PuzzleId>`, then:

1. `background`: the first 6,000 eligible puzzles. Precision is measured here
   (unbiased tag prevalence; about 44 `doubleCheck` puzzles expected, the rarest tag).
2. Per measured tag (§7.3): the first 300 eligible puzzles with that tag that are not
   chosen yet, stratum = the tag. Recall is measured here.

- `test` is committed as `tests/fixtures/puzzles/test.csv` (about 9,300 rows, ≈ 1.4 MB;
  columns `PuzzleId,FEN,Moves,Themes,Stratum`).
- `dev` excludes every test ID, is generated locally into `build/puzzles/dev.csv` and is
  never committed. Only `dev` is used for tuning.

### 7.3 Evaluation

`tactics_puzzle_eval <csv> [--disagreements N <out.tsv>]` (executable in
`tests/tactics`):

- Root = FEN after `Moves[0]`; solver = side to move at root; line = `Moves[1..]`.
- Instances = `position_motifs(root)` ∪ `line_motifs(root, line)`; a tag is predicted
  when an instance mapped to it has `side` = solver.
- Mapping:

  | Lichess tag | Our tags |
  |---|---|
  | `hangingPiece` | `hanging_piece` |
  | `fork` | `fork` |
  | `pin` | `pin_absolute`, `pin_relative` |
  | `skewer` | `skewer` |
  | `discoveredAttack` | `discovered_attack`, `discovered_check` (lichess-puzzler counts discovered checks as discovered attacks) |
  | `discoveredCheck` | `discovered_check` |
  | `doubleCheck` | `double_check` |
  | `backRankMate` | `back_rank_mate` |
  | `capturingDefender` | `removal_of_defender` |
  | `trappedPiece` | `trapped_piece` |
  | `promotion` | `promotion` |

- Per tag: **recall** = TP / tagged puzzles on that tag's stratum (gating, §8.2).
  **Lichess agreement** = TP / (TP + FP) on `background`: the share of puzzles where we
  predict the tag that Lichess also names. Lichess themes name a puzzle's idea, not every
  motif present, so this is printed for information and never gates. Prints a table and
  the mean number of instances per puzzle (all sides, root plus line) on `background`;
  `--disagreements` writes FP and FN puzzles (ID, FEN, moves, themes, our instances).
- CTest `tactics_puzzles` (label `unit`) runs it on `test.csv` and fails below the
  frozen recall targets (§8.2). Before §8 is approved it only prints.

### 7.4 Engine confirmation (precision)

Precision asks whether what we report is real, with Stockfish as the judge
(Captain, 2026-10-19). `tactics_motif_confirm <csv> [--per-tag N] [--jobs J]
[--details <out.tsv>]` (executable in `tests/tactics`, links the engine and analysis
modules):

- **Claim tags** (the motif claims that X wins material): `hanging_piece`, `fork`,
  `skewer`, `pin_relative`, `discovered_attack`, `removal_of_defender`, `trapped_piece`.
  The other tags are exact rule facts (`discovered_check`, `double_check`, `promotion`,
  `back_rank_mate`, `pin_absolute`), checked by unit tests and T fixtures, or fixture-only.
- **Population**: every instance of a claim tag, both sides, root and line (as in §7.3),
  from `background` rows.
- **Sample**: per tag, instances sorted ascending by 64-bit FNV-1a of
  `chesswhy-m2-confirm:<PuzzleId>:<instance>` (instance spelled as in the
  `--disagreements` output: `tag:side:ply:actor>targets`); the first N (default 200, all
  when fewer).
- **Board** B: root after `ply` line moves. X = the motif's side, Y the other. Tracked
  pieces: the motif's actor and targets that are Y pieces standing on B.
- B is checkmate of Y: confirmed. B is stalemate: not confirmed. Otherwise one search on
  B: Stockfish from `assets/`, Threads 1, Hash 64, MultiPV 1, 200,000 nodes (the
  `standard` budget's search B); L = its line.
- Over `walked_plies(B, L.pv)` plies: X's material = `consequence(B, L.pv, L).material`,
  negated when Y is to move at B. Mate for X: `L.mate` set and positive when X is to move
  at B, negative when Y is. **Hit**: an X move in those plies captures a tracked piece
  (tracked pieces follow Y's moves; en passant captures the pawn).
- **Confirmed** iff hit and (mate for X or X's material ≥ 1). Engine-confirmed precision
  = confirmed / sampled. The table also counts the two failure kinds (no hit; hit
  without gain). `--details` writes each sampled instance: PuzzleId, instance, FEN of B,
  pv, outcome.
- `--jobs J` runs J engine processes; every search starts with `ucinewgame`, so results
  do not depend on J and are printed in sample order.
- CTest `tactics_confirm` (label `engine`) runs it on `test.csv` and fails below the
  frozen precision targets (§8.2). Before §8 is approved it only prints.

## 8. Calibration and targets

### 8.1 Calibration (WP6)

- On `dev` only. Allowed changes: thresholds and conditions inside the §4 definitions,
  implemented in `src/tactics`, so that the report contains exactly what is measured
  (no evaluator-only filters). Not allowed: tags, report shape, the sample, the mapping
  table.
- Experiments the brief lists first (Captain, 2026-10-19): `pin_relative` requires that
  exposing `q` wins material (`see` on `q`'s square with `p` removed ≥ 1); a line
  relevance rule (a state motif at ply k ≥ 1 is kept only when a later move of the same
  line moves or captures its actor or a target).
- Measures: recall (§7.3) for every measured tag, engine-confirmed precision (§7.4) for
  claim tags, mean instances per puzzle. A change is kept only if it raises recall or
  confirmed precision of its tag without lowering the other by more than 0.02, does not
  raise mean instances, and breaks no T fixture or F row. Lichess agreement is reported,
  never optimised.
- Every change gets a §15 row with dev numbers before and after.
- Done when recall and confirmed precision on `dev` have stopped improving without
  breaking a T fixture, or after the changes listed in the brief.
- Then the worker runs `test` once and reports dev and test tables side by side.

### 8.2 Targets (Captain's checkpoint)

The orchestrator proposes `target = floor_to_0.05(test value − 0.03)` for recall of
every measured tag (§7.3) and for engine-confirmed precision of every claim tag (§7.4),
with the definitions as calibrated. The Captain approves or adjusts. Then the targets are
frozen in this section and in `tests/tactics`, and `tactics_puzzles` and `tactics_confirm`
gate. A tag whose numbers the Captain finds too low becomes either a fixture-only tag
(like `overloaded_defender`) or a scope proposal; it is never dropped silently.

### 8.3 Audit (not gating)

After the targets are frozen, the orchestrator reviews on `test`, with board and engine
help: per measured tag the first 10 recall misses in file order, classified *detector
wrong*, *Lichess label doubtful* or *unclear*; per claim tag the first 10 unconfirmed
sampled instances in sample order, classified *detector wrong*, *real but the engine
prefers another line* or *unclear*. The receipt reports the counts.

## 9. Fixtures

  actor, targets}` instances, actor and targets as square names (any field may be omitted in

  with at least two positives and two near misses each.

  orchestrator.

  `motifs_include` (pointer to a `motifs` array → list of `{tag, side, actor}`, actor as a
  square name; each listed instance must be present, extra motifs are allowed), added to
  at least five F fixtures, only where the line holds at `quick`, `standard` and `deep`.
- **Frozen at checkpoint 1 (Captain, 2026-10-19):** T01–T41 as committed in `65df1d8`,

## 10. Template

- Under each printed line, one row per motif of that line: tag, actor and targets as
  piece letter plus square, ply, `[rule]`. Example: `fork: N e5 → r c6, k g6 (ply 3) [rule]`.
  Wording is pinned by golden tests.
- Lines that get motif rows: both threats; fine candidates' own lines; for moves that are
  not fine only the refutation; `creates_threat`.
- `position.motifs` is not printed (D8). It stays in the JSON.
